This page compiles key economic statistics for Turkey from official sources, providing a data-driven snapshot of the country's current economic conditions. It serves as a reference for understanding the Turkish economy in global context.

## TL;DR

Turkey's economy presents a complex picture in 2026: it remains the 19th largest economy globally by nominal GDP, with a population of 86 million and a strategic geographic position bridging Europe and Asia. However, persistent inflation, currency volatility, and structural challenges in employment and current account balance continue to shape policy debates.

## Key Context

As economist Daron Acemoglu of MIT notes in his work on institutions and economic performance, "the differences in economic institutions are the major source of differences in economic development" across countries. Turkey's economic trajectory illustrates this: its strategic position, young population, and diversified industrial base provide strong fundamentals, while institutional frameworks around central bank independence, regulatory quality, and rule of law significantly influence its economic outcomes.

The data above reflects official statistics as of July 2026. Economic indicators are subject to revision, and readers should consult the primary sources cited for the most current figures. Turkey's economy remains one of the most closely watched emerging markets, with its unique combination of G20 membership, EU customs union membership, and significant geopolitical positioning.
